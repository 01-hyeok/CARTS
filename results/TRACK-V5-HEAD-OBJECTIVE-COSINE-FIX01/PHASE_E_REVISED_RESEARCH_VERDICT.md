# PHASE E -- Revised Research Interpretation

## E1. Head Error Redundancy -- the corrected picture

The original report's "heads are nearly orthogonal" claim is **wrong and reversed**. Corrected pooled mean cosine: ETTh1_96 **0.844**, ETTh1_720 **0.952**, Weather_96 **0.650**, Weather_720 **0.809** -- all strongly positive, with medians even higher (0.88-0.98) than the means in 3 of 4 cells. **The 5 heads' independent aggregate predictions are strongly REDUNDANT with each other in error space, not complementary and not orthogonal.** Negative-cosine (genuine cancellation) remains rare (0-15% of rows, UNCHANGED by the fix since sign is scale-invariant) -- so while most head pairs mostly agree on their error direction, a real, non-trivial minority of query-channel pairs (most visibly Weather_96, whose 5th-percentile pooled cosine is actually negative, -0.395) do show genuine disagreement. **Is there a meaningful negative correlation anywhere? Only in the tail, never in the typical case.**

## E2. Candidate Diversity vs Error Complementarity -- confirmed distinct, with a sharper number now available

Overlap and (corrected) cosine correlate positively within every pair/cell (Pearson/Spearman UNCHANGED by the fix, confirmed scale/rank-invariant -- e.g. ETTh1_96 Spearman 0.61-0.76). But now that the cosine's own SCALE is correct, the practical conclusion is much stronger: **even the LOWEST-overlap pairs still show substantial positive error cosine.** E.g. ETTh1_96's lowest-overlap pair (H4-H5, overlap=0.237) still has cosine=0.770; Weather_96's lowest-overlap pair (H1-H2, overlap=0.148) still has cosine=0.549. Picking different candidates (low overlap) measurably REDUCES redundancy relative to higher-overlap pairs, but never gets anywhere close to true complementarity (cosine near 0) or cancellation (cosine < 0) in the typical case -- candidate-selection diversity and prediction-error complementarity are related but far from interchangeable, and diversity alone, even at its most extreme observed level in this data, leaves the heads strongly redundant.

## E3. L2 Complementarity Loss -- revised, but not reversed, verdict

**The factual premise behind the original "NOT SUPPORTED" verdict is false** -- it was based on the (buggy) claim that cross-head cosine is already near zero, leaving no redundancy to reduce. The corrected data shows the opposite: substantial redundancy exists (cosine 0.65-0.95). Per the spec's explicit instruction, this fact ALONE does not establish that a Complementarity Loss would help, for several independent reasons that survive the fix:

1. **Within-Mean-Mixture-Set `C` (what actually drives AggMSE) is unaffected by this bug and remains a SEPARATE quantity from the cross-head `R_m` redundancy measured here** (PHASE D3 of the original report's own distinction stands: these are different objects -- one measures interaction among the 5 heads' own independent aggregate predictions, the other measures interaction among the 10 candidates INSIDE the single Mean-Mixture-selected set). High cross-head redundancy does not mechanically imply high within-set `C` is fixable by an explicit cross-head penalty; the within-set `C` already has its own, unaffected numbers (0.22-0.54 across cells, 70-87% of AggMSE) and TRACK-M's own precedent shows an aggregate-aligned loss ALONE already reduces an analogous within-set cross-term substantially, with no separate complementarity term.
2. **Negative-cosine fraction (actual harmful redundancy that a complementarity loss would directly correct) remains small** (0-15%, unaffected by the fix) -- most of the newly-revealed redundancy is heads agreeing on direction, not heads harmfully reinforcing the SAME error in a way that is obviously fixable by a pairwise anti-correlation penalty; a naive `L_comp` could just as easily push already-correlated-but-correct heads apart for no forecasting benefit.
3. **The spec's own degenerate-solution warning remains fully applicable and is, if anything, MORE salient now**: with genuinely high baseline redundancy, a loss that directly minimizes `(1/H)<e_m,e_n>` has an easy, cheap way to reduce that dot product that has nothing to do with genuine complementarity -- inflating `||e_m||`/`||e_n||` while only mildly reducing the cosine (a dot product scales with both magnitude and angle; since the angle is apparently hard to move much per E2, a training process incentivized purely by a shrinking dot product could plausibly find it easier to grow error magnitude than to reduce the data's actual redundancy).
4. **Soft-prediction (this measurement) vs Hard Top-10 (what Stage2 actually sees) interaction may differ**, exactly as the spec warns -- this track measured redundancy between each head's own INDEPENDENT `R_m`, not between the candidates that actually end up inside the real, pooled Mean-Mixture Top-10 set (a weighted blend across heads, not any one head's own picks). No claim is made here that fixing cross-head `R_m` redundancy would change the ACTUAL deployed set's own (unaffected, already-measured) `C_mix`.

**Revised verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (upgraded from NOT SUPPORTED).** The stated justification for dismissing L2 was factually wrong and is retracted. The corrected fact (heads are genuinely redundant) is new, real, and research-worthy -- but it raises a DIFFERENT, more interesting question this track does not resolve: is head redundancy itself a useful target (motivating some diversity-inducing mechanism), separately from whether the SPECIFIC `L_comp` pairwise-dot-minimization formula in the spec is the right tool, given its own flagged degeneracy risk and its uncertain relationship to the within-set `C` term that actually drives AggMSE. This is now an open, not a closed, question -- it should be investigated (e.g., a controlled ablation isolating whether an explicit complementarity term adds anything BEYOND L3 alone, mirroring TRACK-M's own M1-vs-M2-vs-K2 structure) rather than assumed either way.
